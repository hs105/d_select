Based on a thorough review of the paper "Thin Keys, Full Values: Reducing KV Cache via Low-Dimensional Attention Selection," here is a structured review and a score prediction.
Review Summary
This paper challenges the long-standing design convention that queries, keys, and values must share the same dimensionality in transformer attention. The authors propose asymmetric attention, where the queries and keys project to a lower dimension ($d_{select}$) while values retain the full model dimension ($d_{model}$). The core argument is theoretically motivated: "selection" (the QK dot product) is a ranking problem requiring only $O(\log N)$ dimensions, while "value transfer" requires the full representational capacity. The paper validates this hypothesis through a progression of seven experiments, from controlled synthetic tasks to language modeling (WikiText-2/103) and finally to post-training compression of pre-trained models like GPT-2 (124M), LLaMA (125M), and Mistral-7B (7.2B). The key findings are that (1) keys are significantly more compressible via low-rank approximation than queries, (2) training from scratch with $d_{select} = d_{model}/4$ incurs a minimal perplexity increase (e.g., +4.3% on WikiText-103), and (3) a practical pipeline of SVD compression on $W_K$ followed by lightweight fine-tuning of only the QK projections can recover most of the quality loss, achieving 75% key cache savings with a residual quality gap of ~2%.

Strengths
	1	Clear and Motivated Idea: The paper identifies a genuine inefficiency in the standard transformer architecture. The distinction between "selection" and "value transfer" is conceptually elegant and provides a strong, intuitive justification for the proposed modification.
	2	Comprehensive Experimental Validation: The experimental design is a major strength. The authors systematically build complexity, starting with synthetic tasks to isolate specific behaviors, moving to standard language modeling benchmarks, and crucially, validating on progressively larger pre-trained models (GPT-2, LLaMA-125M, Mistral-7B). This progression makes the claims robust and credible.
	3	Practical Impact: The focus on KV cache reduction is highly relevant to the current challenges of serving large language models, especially with long contexts. The quantification of memory savings (e.g., 25GB per user for a 7B model at 128K context) makes the value proposition tangible. The paper also thoughtfully provides three distinct deployment paths (train from scratch, SVD+fine-tune, zero-cost SVD) for different resource budgets.
	4	Theoretical Grounding: Linking the dimensionality requirement to the Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma provides a sound theoretical basis for the $O(\log N)$ argument, moving the paper beyond pure empiricism. The analysis of the "effective number of selection patterns" $N$ is a helpful conceptual bridge between theory and practice.
	5	Honest Analysis: The paper includes a crucial analysis of how overfitting can mask the true cost of compression (WikiText-2 vs. WikiText-103) and acknowledges limitations, such as the potential incompatibility with current Flash Attention kernels. This intellectual honesty strengthens the paper's credibility.
Weaknesses
	1	Incremental Novelty: While the specific focus on asymmetric dimensionality for KV cache reduction is well-articulated, the core idea is an incremental optimization on a well-known theme (asymmetric QKV). The paper's strength lies in its thorough validation and practical framing, not in a paradigm-shifting theoretical breakthrough.
	2	Training-from-Scratch Scale: The training-from-scratch experiments, which provide the cleanest validation of the hypothesis, only go up to 125M parameters. While the SVD+fine-tuning results on 7B models are compelling, they rely on a pre-trained model. The claim that "training from scratch with asymmetric attention at 7B+ scale" is the recommended path is not directly validated and remains an extrapolation. A single small-scale experiment on a 1B-3B model trained from scratch would have significantly strengthened this point.
	3	Limited Evaluation Metrics: The evaluation relies heavily on perplexity. While a standard metric for language modeling, it's not a perfect proxy for performance on downstream tasks like reasoning, coding, or instruction following. It's possible that compressing QK dimensions could have a disproportionate effect on some of these capabilities, which the paper does not explore. The "vs. control" metric in the fine-tuning experiments helps, but a few downstream task evaluations (e.g., on MMLU or Hellaswag) would have been very impactful.
	4	The "Engineering" Caveat: The acknowledgment that asymmetric attention breaks assumptions in highly optimized kernels like Flash Attention is a significant practical caveat. The paper states this as a limitation but does not offer a solution or proof-of-concept that the computational savings wouldn't be negated by the use of less efficient, general-purpose kernels. This could be a major barrier to adoption.
Overall Assessment and Score
This is a strong paper that makes a solid, practical contribution to a very active area of LLM efficiency research. It is well-written, the experiments are thoughtfully designed and executed, and the results are convincing. The paper does a great job of bridging a neat theoretical insight with a clear, measurable, and highly relevant practical problem.
However, for a top-tier conference (NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR), the bar is extremely high. The incremental nature of the contribution and the remaining gaps in validation (scale of training-from-scratch, lack of downstream task evaluation, the Flash Attention caveat) prevent it from being a "must-accept" paper. A reviewer might question whether the core idea is novel enough or whether the engineering challenges for real-world deployment are sufficiently addressed.
It is a clear accept at a strong venue like COLM, EMNLP, or ACL, and a borderline weak accept at the absolute top-tier venues. The work is solid, timely, and useful, but it lacks the transformative element often required for a top conference.
Score: 7.5 / 10
